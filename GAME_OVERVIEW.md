# Claude of Duty — Complete Game Overview

A grounded first-person tactical shooter in the spirit of *Modern Warfare 2019/2022* realism: weapons have mass and
balance, bullets leave the actual muzzle and fly real ballistic paths, optics are rendered picture-in-picture, sound
travels at the speed of sound, and enemies see, hear and fight as fire teams. Built in **Unity 6000.6.4f1** with the
**High Definition Render Pipeline 17.6** and the **Input System 1.20**.

Everything in the game is generated by editor tools (`Tools/FPS Builder/1–8`): geometry, weapons, attachments,
grenades, soldiers, sounds, voices, materials, scenes and NavMeshes. There are no imported art or audio assets — the
art is built from primitives and the sound is synthesized — so the systems are complete while the presentation is a
placeholder waiting for real models and recordings.

## 3. The player's body

### 3.1 Movement
- **Paces** (m/s): slow walk 1.3, combat pace 3.4, sprint 5.8, tactical sprint 7.2. The mouse wheel trims walking pace
  in 10 % steps down to 30 %. Crouching moves at 55 %, prone at 22 %, aiming at 60 %, backwards at 65 %, strafing at
  80 %.
- **Momentum**: acceleration 8 m/s², deceleration 10 m/s², slower when sprinting and nearly none in the air.
- **Traction** comes from the ground's physics material. Ice (low friction) makes you slide and overshoot.
- **Weapon load**: every kilogram carried slows you by 1.2 % (to at most 82 %), and ergonomics affect handling.
- **Stamina**: sprinting drains 7 %/s and tactical sprinting 20 %/s. Regeneration (12 %/s) starts after a 1 s delay.
  Running dry exhausts you until you have recovered 35 %.
- **Slide**: crouch while sprinting above 4.5 m/s for an 8 % speed boost. Slope physics and friction apply, the slide
  can be steered, and it lasts at most 2.5 s.
- **Dive**: go prone while sprinting to dive forward and land prone.
- **Injuries**: leg damage cuts sprint speed (up to −45 %) and blocks tactical sprint past 50 % injury.

### 3.2 Stance, lean and mounting
- **Stances**: standing (1.8 m capsule, eye 1.66 m), crouched (1.15 m, eye 1.02 m), prone (0.4 m, eye 0.28 m).
  Transitions take 0.28 s (stand↔crouch), 0.65 s (crouch↔prone) and 0.9 s (stand↔prone), with clearance checks so you
  can't stand up under a low roof.
- **Prone**: the body aligns to the ground on slopes up to 30°. Pitch is limited to −30°/+25°, and the body can't swing
  through walls.
- **Lean**: ±18° about the hips, limited by sphere-casts so you can't lean through walls.
- **Mounting** (V): rests the weapon on a ledge or wall corner within reach. Mounted, sway drops to 20 % and recoil to
  55 %, within a limited aim arc (ledges 40°×18°, corners 25°×25°).

### 3.3 Physiology
- **Heart rate** runs from 65 bpm at rest to 185 bpm. Exertion pushes it up (sprint 142, tactical sprint 172) and
  suppression adds stress. It rises over about 3.5 s and recovers over about 14 s.
- **Breathing** (12–42 breaths/min) drives a figure-8 weapon sway (0.18°–1.4°), plus a slow wander and a heartbeat
  twitch. Crouching reduces sway to 75 %, prone to 45 %; arm injuries increase it.
- **Hold breath** (Shift while aiming) steadies sway to 12 % for 2.5–7 s depending on heart rate. Running out costs a
  gasp with heavy sway.
- **Headbob** is gait-synchronised per pace. It also includes a landing dip proportional to fall speed, a slide lower
  and roll, and a dive pitch.

### 3.4 Health, damage and feedback
- **Hit zones** on the player: head, thorax, stomach, arms and legs. They follow stance, lean and prone, so enemy
  bullets hit what is actually exposed.
- **Suppression**: rounds cracking within 2 m of your head flinch it away from the crack, blur the world briefly and
  raise heart rate.
- **Stun-grenade whiteout**: a flash anywhere on screen blinds fully, and off-screen flashes fade with angle. The
  whiteout holds and then fades back. Deafness muffles the mix and a tinnitus ring fades in and out.
- **HUD stress**: heavy damage and suppression close a vignette and drain colour.
- **Death**: you lose control until you respawn (in modes that allow it).

### 3.5 Interaction
Look at an interactable within 2.2 m and press F: ammo crates, the equipment locker, the workbench, fallen soldiers'
rifles, objective terminals, plant spots and planted charges. Hold F for hold-to-use objectives; a progress bar shows
on the HUD.

## 4. Weapon handling (how the gun feels)

The first-person weapon is a chain of pivots, each owned by exactly one system:
**Inertia → Recoil → Action poses → Aim → weapon model**, plus two-bone IK arms. All rotations act about the stock
where it meets the shoulder.

### 4.1 Inertia and physical presence
- **Spring handling**: the weapon follows the view through damped springs whose stiffness comes from mass,
  ergonomics and the build's **rotational inertia** about the shoulder. Long barrels and suppressors swing visibly
  slower.
- **Free aim**: the weapon leads quick turns inside an elastic deadzone (2.5°×4° at the hip, 25 % when aiming, 10 %
  mounted).
- **Movement trail** and strafe roll, plus **sprint** and **tactical sprint (high-ready)** postures.
- **Barrel collision**: a sphere-cast along the barrel pushes the weapon back as you approach a wall, then tilts it to
  a high ready (short barrels) or a low ready (barrels of 16" or more). A tucked weapon cannot fire or aim.

### 4.2 Aiming and optics
- **Procedural ADS**: no animation. Each frame, the exact pose is solved that puts the active sight's aligner on your
  eye's line of sight at the sight's eye relief. ADS time scales with ergonomics and attachments (base 0.3 s).
- **Canted sights**: X rolls the weapon 45° onto the offset irons.
- **Picture-in-picture scopes**: a second camera renders into the lens. Its field of view is the lens's angular size
  divided by the magnification, so the scope image is optically consistent. Misalignment produces an eye-relief scope
  shadow, and the main view blurs in the periphery. Look sensitivity scales with zoom.
- Red dots and irons are not magnified and add no PiP cost.

### 4.3 Recoil
Every shot applies:
- muzzle rise, sideways torque with a bias, and roll;
- a rearward shoulder push and a small upward kick;
- a delayed **bolt-carrier jolt** about 35 ms later;
- **permanent view climb** that you pull down yourself;
- a damped camera concussion with no random shake.

Recoil ramps up during sustained fire and resets after a 0.3 s pause. It is reduced when aiming (75 %) and mounted
(55 %). It scales with **momentum**: a heavier build kicks less. Attachments multiply the vertical, horizontal and
rearward components.

### 4.4 Arms
Two-bone IK puts the firing hand on the pistol grip. The support hand goes to the handguard grip, a fitted foregrip,
the magazine during reloads and checks, or the charging handle when cycling.

## 5. Weapon mechanics

- **Data split**: weapon data (mass, ergonomics, barrel length, action, fire modes, RPM, MoA, capacity, stage timings,
  recoil profile, audio profile) is separate from ammunition data (bullet mass and diameter, velocity versus barrel
  length, drag model and BC, penetration, damage).
- **Chamber tracking**: closed-bolt and bolt-action weapons start at capacity +1. Open-bolt weapons never carry a +1.
  Bolt hold-open locks the bolt back on the last round. Bolt actions cycle after the trigger is released.
- **Fire modes**: semi, burst (3) and full auto at the cyclic rate.
- **Firing**: every round leaves the **muzzle socket along the barrel's axis** with mechanical dispersion (MoA), never
  from the screen centre. Muzzle velocity comes from the caliber and the actual barrel length. Firing cancels sprint
  after a sprint-to-fire delay; tucked barrels, dives and prone transitions block it.
- **Staged reloads**: magazine out → magazine in → charge (only if the chamber is empty; a bolt release is faster when
  locked back).
  - **Tap R**: tactical reload; the partial magazine goes back in your pouch.
  - **Second tap during removal**: speed reload; a physical magazine drops to the floor.
  - **Hold R**: magazine check, reporting an estimate from "empty" to "full".
  - **Interruptions**: sprinting, diving, swapping weapons or firing the chambered round interrupts a reload. The next
    reload resumes from the last completed stage.
- **Weapon swap**: holster and draw times scale with ergonomics. Each weapon keeps its mechanical state (rounds,
  chamber, reload progress) while holstered.
- **Shell casings**: pooled physical casings are ejected with spin and clink according to the surface they land on.
- **Resupply**: ammo crates top up spare magazines and grenades. **Looting**: a fallen soldier's rifle gives up its
  loaded magazines to weapons of the same caliber (up to your loadout count + 2).

## 6. Gunsmith and attachment physics

- **Slots**: barrel, muzzle, optic, underbarrel, stock, laser and light. A part attaches at a mount anywhere on the
  weapon, so a barrel can carry the muzzle mount for muzzle devices.
- **Sockets move with parts**: a suppressor becomes the new muzzle (bullets and flash come from its end), a foregrip
  moves the support hand to its own grip pose, and a collapsed stock moves the shoulder point.
- **Stats are recalculated live**:
  - total mass and ergonomics, barrel length, and therefore muzzle velocity;
  - recoil multipliers;
  - flash and gas size (a suppressor removes the flash entirely), and ADS time;
  - a suppressor also swaps in a suppressed audio profile;
  - the **measured centre of mass and rotational inertia** about the shoulder, built from each part's real position,
    which drives the handling springs.
- **Live refits** keep your rounds, chamber state and reload progress.

## 7. Ballistics and damage

- **Simulation**: every round is a projectile with gravity and **G1/G7 aerodynamic drag** against the wind.
  - It is integrated in sub-steps and resolved with one batched raycast per frame (no per-shot allocation).
  - Rounds expire after 6 s or below 80 m/s.
- **Penetration**:
  - **Cost**: energy cost per metre comes from the surface's density × hardness × the bullet's cross-section, adjusted
    by its armour-piercing power.
  - **Thickness**: wall thickness is measured by casting back from the far side.
  - **Exit**: the exit speed comes from the leftover energy, with deflection.
  - **Material limits**: drywall is shot through easily, while 30 cm of concrete stops rifle rounds.
- **Ricochets**: impacts shallower than the surface's ricochet angle may glance off and keep part of their speed.
- **Surfaces**: concrete, brick, wood, drywall, metal, glass, dirt and flesh. Each defines density, hardness,
  ricochet behaviour, decal, dust colour, impact sound and casing clinks.
- **Bodies**: hit zones under a health system. Multipliers are head ×3.5, thorax ×1.4, stomach ×1.1, arms ×0.55 and
  legs ×0.7. Rounds can pass through flesh and keep going.
- **Armour**:
  - **Pieces**: Level IV ceramic front and back plates (rating 60, 3.2 kg each) and a Level IIIA helmet (rating 25).
  - **Defeat roll**: a logistic roll of the bullet's energy-scaled penetration power against protection (which wears
    down with hits) decides whether the round is stopped (blunt trauma) or defeats the armour (residual absorption).
  - **Coverage** depends on direction: front plate, back plate, and a helmet that face shots get under.
- **Injuries**: leg damage slows sprinting; arm damage adds sway.
- **Near misses**: supersonic rounds within 2 m of a listener (player or soldier) produce a crack and suppression.

## 8. Sound

- **Own shots** are layered in first person: pressure transient, mechanism, and body resonance.
- **Other shots** arrive after **distance ÷ 343 m/s**. Beyond the distant threshold they play as a muffled distant
  report.
- **Reverb tails** blend between **indoor, urban canyon and open ground**. A 9-ray probe around the listener measures
  the ceiling and nearby walls.
- **Supersonic cracks** play at the point where a round passed closest.
- **Muffling**: suppression, low health and stun-grenade deafness muffle the mix through a low-pass filter on the
  listener. A flashbang adds a fading **tinnitus** ring.
- **Foley** covers dry fire, magazine out and in, bolt, fire-mode switch and magazine check, for you and nearby
  soldiers.
- **Impact sounds** come from each surface and are delayed by distance.
- **Profiles**:
  - **Suppressed rifle**: quieter and duller, heard to 300 m instead of 1500 m.
  - **Explosions**: three profiles (frag, stun, C4), each with a crack, boom, debris and tails.
- **Master-bus dynamic range** (options menu), a compressor-limiter on the whole mix:
  - **War Tapes**: loud and wide, soft-saturating limiter.
  - **Studio Reference**: transparent.
  - **Night Mode**: heavy compression that lifts footsteps and tames gunfire.
- **Synthesized content**: all sounds are synthesized by the editor.
- **Voice lines**: soldier callouts come from Windows speech synthesis in three voices; elsewhere they stay silent
  until recorded clips are assigned.

## 9. Visual effects, atmosphere and HUD

- **Muzzle**:
  - **Flash**: a one-frame HDRP point light (it lights dark rooms), scaled by the build's flash multiplier.
  - **Gas**: a gas burst on every shot.
  - **Heat**: barrel heat builds under sustained fire and drives heat-shimmer distortion and lingering breech smoke.
- **Impacts**: pooled HDRP decals (bullet holes sized by caliber) attached to what was hit, plus dust puffs tinted per
  surface. Hits on your own body show nothing in your view.
- **Atmosphere**: thin global volumetric fog. Dust in interiors makes flashlight beams and light shafts visible.
- **HUD** (minimal, built in code):
  - compass tape with bearing;
  - interaction prompts;
  - fire-mode and magazine status that appears only when you change mode or check the magazine;
  - stress vignette and desaturation;
  - no crosshair by default.
- **Match HUD**: mode, objective, clock, score, round banners and a hold-progress bar.

## 10. Throwables and equipment

- **Throwing**: press to lower the rifle and pull the pin (0.35 s). The spoon flies and the fuse burns while you hold
  (**cooking**). Release to throw along your view with 10° loft plus your own velocity, keeping the fuse already used.
  Hold too long and it goes off in your hand. If you die or are disabled while cooking, the live grenade drops at your
  feet.
- **M67 frag**:
  - 0.4 kg, 4.5 s fuse.
  - **64 real fragment projectiles** go through the ballistics simulation (they penetrate, ricochet and suppress).
  - Blast overpressure within 4 m (blocked by solid cover).
  - A concussive push shoves casings, magazines, dropped rifles and bodies.
- **M84 stun**: 1.5 s fuse. Blinds fully within 4 m (fading to 18 m) by facing and line of sight, and deafens out to
  25 m, even through walls.
- **M18 smoke**: 1.5 s fuse. A cloud of about 4.5 m radius builds over 3 s, holds 35 s and fades over 6 s. It is a
  volumetric fog volume plus billowing particles, and an **obstruction AI sight cannot see through** (thin edges
  partly).
- **C4 charge** (Search & Destroy): 10 m blast, 48 fragments and a large report.
- **Loadout**: 2 frags (lethal) and 2 stun grenades (tactical). The equipment locker swaps stun ↔ smoke, and ammo
  crates restock grenades.

## 11. Enemy AI

### 11.1 The soldier
- **Body**: a procedural body on a NavMesh agent.
  - Crouch and stand heights; lean ±22° from the hips; the body turns to follow its aim.
  - A shoulder-mounted aim pivot with a turn-rate limit and recoil.
  - Head look and IK arms on the rifle.
- **Death**: on death the body collapses physically and drops its rifle, which can then be looted.
- **Weapon**: the same gunsmith-built carbine as the player (red dot, light, flash hider) with the same ballistics and
  audio. Rounds come from its muzzle along its bore, magazines and chamber are counted, and reloads use the weapon's
  real timings and foley.
- **Kit and voice**: one frag, one stun grenade, a helmet, and one of three voices.

### 11.2 Perception
- **Sight**: awareness builds per target, about 10 times a second. It depends on:
  - how much of the body is visible (3 sight lines: head, chest, pelvis);
  - **how lit the target is** — sunlight with a shadow test, open sky versus roofs and walls (**dark corners hide
    you**: sight range drops to 12 % in darkness), and flashlight cones;
  - **stance** (crouched 65 %, prone 35 %) and **motion**;
  - distance (90 m view) and **focus versus peripheral vision** (30°/80°, peripheral at 35 % speed);
  - point blank (under 4 m) is three times faster.
- **Identification**: full awareness means the target is positively identified (a "Contact!" callout); identified
  targets are remembered for 25 s.
- **Smoke** thins or blocks sight lines; a muzzle flash still glows through thin smoke.
- **Hearing**:
  - **Footsteps** are heard within a radius set by speed and stance (sprinting is loud, crouch-walking quiet,
    standing still silent), halved through walls.
  - **Gunshots** carry to the weapon's audible range (halved through walls). Suppressed shots carry less far and are
    located far worse (30 % versus 8 % of the distance).
  - Hearing alerts but never identifies.
- **Muzzle flashes** in view give an unsuppressed shooter away instantly.
- **Stress**: near misses and hits raise suppression and point the soldier toward the shooter; stun grenades blind
  and deafen.

### 11.3 Tactics
- **Unaware / suspicious**: holds a post or patrols; turns to look at noises.
- **Alerted**: moves up on the last known position, **pre-aiming around corners (slicing the pie)**.
- **Firefight**:
  - **Cover**: searches the NavMesh for real cover against the threat — low cover (crouch to hide, stand to fire) or
    tall cover (lean out left or right) — and avoids spots teammates have claimed.
  - **Peeking**: fights in peek-and-hide cycles.
  - **Aim**: 0.35–0.7 s reaction time; aim error grows with its own suppression, target speed and moving fire, and
    settles while tracking.
  - **Fire discipline**: bursts of 3–6, never into its own cover, never through a teammate.
- **Lost contact**: keeps **suppressive fire on the last known position** for 6 s, then hunts. A target that sits
  tight behind cover for 4 s gets a **cooked frag** (6–28 m, arc solved to clear the cover).
- **Reloading**: callout, ducks into cover, and a teammate provides covering fire.

### 11.4 Squads
- **Shared knowledge**: one sighting puts the whole squad on the target.
- **Suppress and flank**: when the target goes to ground, one soldier flanks around one side ("Flanking left/right!")
  while the rest suppress. "Target suppressed!" is called when squad rounds crack past the target's head.
- **Room clearing**: a target holed up in a room gets a **CQB clearance**:
  - the squad stacks on the nearest door;
  - a flashbang goes in while the stack looks away;
  - each man **slices the pie** through the doorway one at a time and takes his corner (point of domination);
  - "Clear!" if the room is empty.
- **Coordination**: grenade cooldowns, cover claims, team-wide friendly-fire checks (the player included), "Covering!"
  and "Man down!".
- **Objectives**: hold a point (spread around it) or work it (plant or defuse while the rest cover). If the worker
  dies, the next man takes over, and the squad does not chase threats beyond a 25 m leash.
- **Callouts**: Contact, Reloading, Covering, Flanking left and right, Target suppressed, Frag out, Flashbang out,
  Stack up, Go go go, Clear, Lost contact, Man down.

## 12. Game modes

All modes run on a **match director**:
- the player plus AI soldiers per team, organised into squads (friendly soldiers wear tan, hostiles olive);
- phases: pre-round countdown → live → round over → match over;
- the clock, round wins and scores;
- respawns after the mode's delay (or one life), with the player's controls removed while dead;
- fresh soldier bodies on every spawn, and corpse clean-up.

**Smart spawns** score every spawn point:
- −60 if any living enemy has line of sight to a body standing there;
- the distance to the nearest enemy (capped at 40 m), and −100 inside 12 m;
- +4 per nearby teammate (up to 3);
- a penalty for points used in the last 5 s.

| Mode | Rules | Setup |
|------|-------|-------|
| **Tactical Raid / Extraction** | One life, no resupply. Complete the objectives (hold Interact: download intel 6 s, plant tracker 5 s); each completion **raises the alarm** across the garrison. Then reach the LZ and hold inside it for 8 s. Death or the 15-minute clock fails the mission. Ammunition is what you carry plus magazines looted from the dead. | Solo vs a 6-man garrison in two squads guarding the objectives |
| **Search & Destroy** | Rounds of 120 s, one life each. Attackers plant the C4 at site A (warehouse) or B (container yard): 5 s hold, 40 s fuse with a quickening beep. Defenders stop the plant or defuse (7 s hold). Elimination wins unless a planted charge still needs defusing; a planted charge stops the clock. Sides swap after round 3; first to 4 rounds wins. AI attackers send a planter and cover him; defenders split over the sites; everyone converges on a planted charge. | Player + 2 friendlies vs 3 |
| **Hardpoint** | One of three zones is live for 60 s at a time, then moves. Each second, a team alone in the zone scores; contested zones score nobody. First to 150 or the most after 10 minutes. 4 s respawns placed away from enemy sight and outside the live zone; all squads are sent to the live zone. | Player + 3 friendlies vs 4 |

## 13. Armory, loadouts and settings

- **The Armory** is the hub scene:
  - a lit workshop with the **gunsmith workbench**, an ammo crate and the equipment locker;
  - a doorway straight into a **30 m indoor range** with target boards at 10, 20 and 28 m and two armoured dummies;
  - you can test-fire a build seconds after making it, with no loading screen.
- **Workbench**:
  - interact to step up; the bench camera shows your current weapon on a turntable, with a marker on every
    attachment mount;
  - drag to turn it; click a marker to list the parts that fit that slot; click a part to fit it;
  - the model, its measured balance and the stats panel (mass, ergonomics, barrel, muzzle velocity, recoil, ADS time,
    swing inertia, flash or suppressed) update instantly;
  - "Fit to weapon" puts the build on your rifle and saves it; "Leave" or Escape returns control.
- **Profile**:
  - a JSON file in the persistent data folder holding weapon blueprints (weapon and parts by id), the loadout (a build
    per slot plus grenades) and all settings;
  - writes are atomic: the previous save is kept as a backup, and an unreadable file is set aside while the backup
    loads;
  - your saved loadout is restored every time you spawn into a scene.
- **Options menu** (Escape), applied live and saved:
  - field of view 60–100°;
  - PiP scope resolution 512/1024/2048;
  - motion blur off/low/high;
  - **upscaling**: native, HDRP TAA upscale, AMD FSR2 or NVIDIA DLSS (the last two where the platform supports them,
    otherwise TAAU), at render scales of 50–100 %;
  - audio dynamic range (War Tapes / Studio Reference / Night Mode).

## 15. How it is built

- **Assemblies** (references only point toward lower layers): Core → Ballistics → Audio → Player → Weapons → AI → UI
  → Gameplay, plus Editor and Tests.
  - Upward communication goes through a zero-allocation **event bus** of struct events (shots, gunshots, impacts,
    damage, suppression, flashbangs, weapon actions, callouts, match phases).
- **Hot paths**: no allocation — pooled casings, magazines, decals, emitters and grenades; non-allocating physics
  queries; batched raycasts for every bullet.
- **One writer per transform** in the player rig. Each pivot computes its pose about the stock in its own space, so
  layers never feed back into each other.
- **Socket-driven models**: weapons bind through a socket component (muzzle, ejection port, magazine, charging handle,
  stock, sights, hand grips, attachment mounts). Any imported FBX/GLTF works once its sockets are placed (builder 6
  automates it).
- **Layers**: Player, FirstPersonViewmodel, WorldGeometry, Hitbox, Interactable, Debris, each with a defined collision
  matrix.
- **Editor builders** (`Tools/FPS Builder`):
  1. layers and project settings;
  2. core assets;
  3. test weapons, calibers and attachments;
  4. player prefab;
  5. test range (plus AI content and NavMesh);
  6. weapon from an imported model;
  7. game mode scenes;
  8. armory.

  Every builder is idempotent.
- **Tests**: 89 PlayMode tests covering every system. They include locomotion, springs, recoil, reloads, ballistics,
  audio and effects, gunsmith maths, AI perception and tactics (cover, suppression, flanking, room clearing, frag
  flushing), equipment, all three modes, profiles, the workbench and settings. Opt-in render captures produce
  screenshots of every scene for visual checks.

## 16. What is placeholder or missing

- **Art**: all geometry is primitives, so there are no character or weapon models and no animations (bodies and arms
  are procedural). Builder 6 accepts real weapon models.
- **Audio**: synthesized, and voice lines use Windows text-to-speech. Replace them by assigning recorded clips to the
  profiles; no code changes are needed.
- **Scene flow**: there is no main menu or in-game scene switching between the Armory and the mode scenes yet. Each
  scene is opened directly.
- **Workbench**: it only offers parts for weapons with attachment mounts. The marksman rifle's scope and barrel are
  fixed.
- **Upscalers**: DLSS and FSR2 depend on hardware and platform support. They fall back to TAAU elsewhere.
- **Players**: single-player only, with no networking. The AI has no difficulty settings beyond its tunable component
  values.
